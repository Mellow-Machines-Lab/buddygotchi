The model: Doing, Mood, Moment

Everything Boop shows is one of three layers on the board, and everything it says goes out on one of three channels. The words here are the ones the code, the prompt and the docs use (the Words table in CLAUDE.md has each one, the words it replaced and the names kept on the wire).

Four ideas hold the rest up. The agents: sessions, turns, calls, checks, helpers, to-dos and needs you. You: what you do to Boop (touch, words, presence, the button). Time, two clocks: how long (working or resting, the tiers) and what day (the part of the day, Boop's 5:00 day, special days, growth). Tags and dressing: tags are facts true now (Friday, busy, a party), and dressing is how Doing shows them (a hat, shades, the sun, faster typing).

The rules

  1. Three authors, one shape. A Moment is made by the board (a touch, played at once), the rules (a turn's or session's start, the day's greeting, a stage's celebration, onboarding's acts) or Jev. The board's and the rules' Moments carry no emotion of their own: they show the mood. Onboarding's finale is the one that does: it cheers in happy. When Jev can't be reached, the shrug is sent in the mood Boop is in.
  2. Boop's own beats. An idle check-in, a musing or an ask is a Moment whose event is Boop's own. Jev may always answer none. An idle check-in needs you at the Mac and recent activity; with no agent open it comes at twice the personality's gap (TranscriptView.noAgentsGapTimes). A musing comes at most once an hour (Signs.musingEveryMs); an ask at most once an hour and three a day, six on Chatty, none on Quiet (Asks); a check-in every 15–30 minutes on Normal (the personality's pace), 30–60 on Quiet and 10–15 on Chatty (Talk.pace), and only within two hours of something happening.
  3. A mood change is seen as soon as Boop shows its mood. The state carries the mood, and the face crosses to it. On the gel the mood graph's bridge plays at once, under a Moment that has no emotion of its own. A Moment with its own emotion holds it, and the bridge runs from that emotion when it ends. Asleep, with no app or under the needs-you screen there is no bridge: the new mood's art is there when Boop wakes or the screen goes. On the CYD a dissolve plays when the new mood's design differs.
  4. Emotion within mood. A Moment's emotion is free within the mood's steering (a grumpy Boop's grudging proud at a win). It lasts the Moment's expression, then Doing shows in the mood again.
  5. One priority, top first: listening and its reply; needs you; Moments; Doing.
    • Listening cuts a playing Moment and drops the ones waiting. A reply plays over needs you.
    • A new needs-you cuts the playing Moment with its voice, sign and ask; Moments waiting are refused as they reach the front.
    • Moments play in the order they arrive (LinkKit's queue): at most four wait (the least time left goes first), each at most its time to live, and a newer one replaces a resting one only when nothing waits. The rules' Moments play only when nothing busy plays, else they're dropped.
    • Doing changes under a Moment and shows when it ends.
  6. Two sizes, two facts. Jev's size gives a Moment its loops (1 to 4) and its voice's kind (a sound, a word, a phrase). A turn's length gives its finish's size, by the turn's tier: small under tier 2, medium from 2, big from 4, biggest from 5; a finish bigger than small plays once. A finish with no turn (a stage reached) is sized by its loops, and a sad Boop's finish is small.
  7. Several agents, one Doing. Working if any agent works; else asleep when there are none, or when all have rested past dozing off (an hour by day, the last tier at night) and none waits on you; else resting. Needs you is the screen on top, not a Doing. The activity shown is that of the working session with the latest event; within a session the highest wins, in the order testing, delegating, terminal, searching, analysing, a tool, waiting, planning. A higher activity shows at once and a lower one after 1.5 s; a call quiet for 20 s shows as waiting. The strip counts the rest, the to-do shown is the longest turn's, and the drawer lists every agent.
  8. Buddies. Each buddy has its own Doing (its agents: those its name was said to, else it watches the fewest), its own Mood and its own prompt; they share the transcript. One buddy's Moment is the others' event, until ten replies in a row. The written memory is the first buddy's: only its prompt has MEMORY, and every buddy's CONTEXT has your company and the day.
  9. Silence takes the voice. Silent (a hush) takes only the voice; the emotion, the size and the signs go on. Quiet also takes the voice's topics and swears, offers no asks, and no sign for a calm prompt that doesn't name Boop. Face down is do not disturb: Moments are refused (but listening), the voice, sign and ask showing end, and the screen dims; Doing goes on, and the needs-you screen still shows bright.
  10. A work act is Doing. A command Boop plays out by rule (a push on a rocket), for as long as it runs: it's true in state (wact), so the board times only its own beats, and the result ends it. The full act plays when its kind hasn't for 30 minutes or it's the first of the day, else only its low sign. One at a time; a needs-you or listening holds it out of state, and it comes back where it got to. What Boop says over it is Jev's, by the same rules as anything else: its scene never waits for Jev.
  11. The work stream is Doing. A word coming forward is a do (call) queued next with 1.5 s to live, with no emotion or voice. It ends as soon as it starts, so it never holds a Moment up, and it is sent only while the stream shows (not under needs you or listening). In a full queue the least time left goes first, so a word never pushes out a Moment with longer to wait.

The lane

The bottom band (Behaviour::laneBubble and caption) shows the bubble while the voice plays, or an ask's two answers when nothing needs you; and the narration's words as a caption, small and grey on its last line, under the bubble or alone, never as a bubble (under needs you, only a reply's; none while an ask's answers show). With neither, the strip: the needs-you name first, then a finish's names, then the counts. Once a finish's words end, by 3 s at the latest, the gel's low sign takes the lane instead and names its thread, for the 5 s it lingers; Boop's whole face stays clear.

How Boop works, on one page

One ordinary event: Claude finishes a turn on "api".

  1. The hook. Claude Code's Stop hook runs agent-hook, which writes one JSON line to Boop's socket and never waits.
  2. Intake. The Mac reads the agent's last message into a reading (its kind, tone, length and up to three short phrases) and lets the words go. The finish and the reading go into today's transcript file.
  3. The rules, at once. The core updates the sessions and sends the board a new state: Doing, the mood, the counts, the to-do list and the tags. The work stream sends its own word for the finish ("done").
  4. The view. TranscriptView writes the event as a line for the brain, counts runs (quick wins, failing checks) and decides whether it wakes the brain.
  5. The brain, later. Jev reads the prompt (below) and answers every question in one request: the mood, the emotion, the move, the size, what Boop says, and sometimes a sign. All multiple choice.
  6. The outputs. The mood output keeps or moves the mood. The react output applies its code rules (Talk, hush, needs you), picks a recorded take, adds the narration, and sends a do to the board.
  7. The board. LinkKit's queue plays it when the Moment before it ends. The board picks the design's variation, sizes a finish by the turn's length, plays the take, and reports ended. Between Moments it deals its pastimes by the tiers.

Watching it: in debug mode every step above lands in debug.jsonl, the transcript's events, the view's lines, each pass with its prompt and answers, and every message sent to the board. boopctl dash reads only that file: Boop now with the CONTEXT Jev last read, then the mood and memory, the rules' Moments and Jev's, side by side.

Where decisions are made: agent-hooks (which hooks count, needs you); the prompt reader (readings); the core (Doing, needs you, the tags and tiers, hush, the start's Moment, BOOT's line, the drawer); the work stream (which word plays); the view (lines, runs, what wakes the brain, the check-ins); Jev (the mood and every other Moment); the react output's code (silences, the signs offered, takes); the board (the queue, variations, pastimes, what a touch is); and the pack (the mood graph's prose, every look and sound).

Memory

  • The transcript is every event, a file a day (Transcript). The view rebuilds what the brain hears from its last 24 hours.
  • Two readers of the transcript, each with its checkpoint. The day's (MemoryReader) keeps today as it goes, a calendar day from 5:00 that triggers nothing: your company (when you last touched or talked to Boop, came back), the tallies for CONTEXT's Today row, and the XP against each day's cap. The dream's (DreamReader) keeps everything since the last dream, however long: the tallies and the journal of notable lines.
  • A dream updates the memory from everything since its checkpoint (Memory.dream): it makes the dream, the span in tags and a sentence, which Boop tells the next time you show up ("Boop dreamed about Thursday to Saturday: …"); it runs the dream's write; and it moves the checkpoint. Hooks reach only a running app, so a Mac closed for days dreams once of what was recorded. It dreams when you press d on the dashboard; by itself once a sleep, after Boop has been asleep with no agent working for 20 minutes (you away, or dozed off); and at once when it hasn't in two days. Each only with something new, one at a time. D on the dashboard previews it, the dream and the write's edits, and keeps nothing.
  • The written memory, memory.md, is short notes in three sections and a line for each dream's day. Apple Intelligence edits it at each dream from the journal (MemoryEdits). You can read and edit it, and the popover's What Boop knows pane shows it read-only, through the memory store. Jev reads it as MEMORY. Only MemoryStore reads and writes the files.

The wish

Boop's want, about one a day by its steering: pet, chat or work, each met by what you do anyway. Jev picks when, as one more option in the sign question at an ask's moments; code holds the rest (Core+Wish.swift): one open, work only while no agent works, chat only once push-to-talk is authorized, offered on Quiet too with no sound. It's kept like the hush, as wish_start and wish_end (met, gaveup, closed), read back on launch. What meets it does so at once, by a rule, with the board's wish_met, and Jev then hears it: for a pet, any touch on its face (Touch.meetsPetWish: a stroke, a poke, a nose boop, a tickle, the wrong-way stroke; a touch's own answer stays, under the heart), for a chat push-to-talk, for work a prompt you send or a request you answer. While it waits the board shows a thought bubble (state.wish) and the wait counts only while you're here, it hasn't dozed off and nothing needs you. The Mac's sleep and Boop not running don't count (Core.wishTickGapMs), an away counts up to its since, and a launch reads the wait back from the events. With no Mac the board takes the bubble down, and during a full act. Asleep only because no agent is open isn't dozing: it may still want something. CONTEXT's You row says how long, and turning sad or lonely at about an hour is Jev's, by steering. Code takes it down at three hours (wish_gone, which wakes Jev) and closes it quietly at 5:00. Until you've met a kind once, the wish shows how: the pack's longer sign, and the bubble's icon moving (state.wish_how), so the board keeps nothing; the "done once" is wish.<kind> in the key-value store, as the asks' records are. Wish lines are notable in the journal, so the written memory carries the pattern.

Narration and asks

Narration is why the buddy does what it does, in plain words (Narration). The Mac renders it from the event the brain answered, never the brain: a sentence for the popover and the debug log, and a few words for the board's lane (the do's reason).

An ask is a question the buddy holds up with two answers you tap in the lane, from the pack's asks.md (Asks). It never needs an answer, and the agents never wait on one. The board says how it ended (ev ask), and the line goes into HISTORY; a question marked keeps is asked only once, ever.

The rules that are easy to break

  • Decisions and effects are separate. The core and the brain decide. Outputs (mood, react) carry out effects and check their own rules.
  • Everything goes through the transcript. Hooks, pokes, check-ins and everything the rules and outputs do are raw events in one shape; the view folds them into what the brain hears (app/BoopKit/Core/TranscriptView.swift). Anything else is a log line.
  • The packages stay apart. agent-hooks, MellowHarness and LinkKit know nothing of Boop or of each other. Boop translates between them in a few places (documentation/architecture.html, Where the pieces join), and anything Boop-specific stays in app/ or firmware/.
  • The harness is generic. MellowHarness takes events, asks every output's questions in one request and hands each output its own answers. It never reads an event's facts, picks what Boop says or talks to the device.
  • Only Voice knows what Boop can say. Only the memory store reads and writes the memory files. Only LinkKit knows Bluetooth or USB: on the Mac its DeviceLink and transports, with Boop's vocabulary on them in app/BoopKit/DeviceLink/BoopDevice.swift; on the board its device library, which firmware/src/main.cpp feeds the serial port's messages.
  • The brain is never on the screen's path. Rules keep the screen true at once (the look, "needs you", the tap's poke, the rules' Moments). Every Moment, a finished turn's included, is the brain's, later or not at all.
  • The brain is Jev: multiple choice only. It answers questions about the prompt, in plain text; there's no free text. Keep questions few, with options that say what they're not.
  • The steering files are read-only at runtime. The pack's steering/ is the single source, and the app bundles it at build time (Package.swift copies it into the app's resources).
  • No code, commands, tool output, file contents or agent transcripts go to the brain. Of your prompt and of the agent's last message only the reading goes, each with up to three short phrases of it; the only other words are what you say to Boop on push-to-talk, cut short. Both texts stay on the Mac: app/BoopKit/Prompt/ scrubs each, reads it (with Apple Intelligence on macOS 26 while it's on, else by counting only) and passes on only its reading; nothing keeps them.
  • "Needs you" and the screen priority are plain rules in the core.
  • The device renders, reports, and decides when a Moment plays (LinkKit's queue), never what Boop does: the Mac sends each thing to play at once and never times it. It receives the same messages over Bluetooth and USB. Its drawing code stays independent of the display library, so the simulator and a later ESP-IDF + LVGL port can reuse it.
  • The Mac's face is the board's firmware. The popover and onboarding show the gel through the pack's mirror of the board (characters/boop/mac/mirror/board.js, in SlimeBuddy), the firmware built to WebAssembly and checked in: a change to what the gel board draws, here or in the pack, rebuilds it (firmware/tools/mirror.sh) into the pack, pushed with the change, and make -C internal mirror fails until it does. It's fed what the board gets and never answers.

The shape

Boop is a Mac app plus a small screen on your desk. Data flows one way: from your coding agents (Claude Code, Codex), through the Mac app, to the device. Boop only watches and reports. It never approves or blocks anything an agent does. Three packages handle the general parts (reading agent hooks, asking the multiple-choice model, talking to the device), and they know nothing about Boop or each other. Boop's own code connects them.

Who the creature is, its moods, prompts, faces and voice, is a character pack, which the engine never reads the meaning of (characters/CHARACTER.md). Each of Boop's characters is a repository of its own, its pack beside this one as its engine/ submodule: SlimeBuddy, the gel slime, and PixelBuddy, Pixel. Only what's a character's own goes there; everything every character shares is here.

  8. Jev's answers
  9. Outputs and Voice

    mood keeps or changes Boop's mood, and records it in the log, where the core reads it for the screen. react asks the same four things of every Moment: its emotion, its move (a finished turn's outcome, or to what you said, its reply), its size, which sets how long the emotion holds and how big the take, and what it says. It says nothing while Boop is hushed (you told it to be quiet, or pressed Hush: the core keeps until when, the board zips its mouth), nor, on Quiet, at a small Moment or a finish. Otherwise it picks a recorded voice clip (a take) that fits the emotion, to what you said the reply's own words ("Yep", "Tsk"), at a medium Moment or bigger sometimes with a sound of the same meaning before a word ("Ugh... Oops"), and, for your prompt's reading, maybe a sign to hold up (a caption from the pack's signs.md, often your own words), then sends the Moment to the device, which picks the animation's variation: a finish says the tier its turn reached (turn), and the gel sizes it by that and how long it's held, from a hop to the whole screen. A finish, or a face for a finished turn, names whose turn it was (who), which the board marks while it plays and 3 s after: the gel's rim glows and a ticket naming the thread hangs from the top. Every Moment also carries its narration, why it plays, rendered on the Mac from the line the brain answered (Narration), never written by the brain: the sentence goes to the transcript, and a few words of it (reason: "stroked", "away 12 h") to the board, which shows them in the lane while nothing is said, unless the strip already names the finished turn. The popover says what last moved the mood the same way. At a finish, an idle check-in or a lonely one, react may ask you something: the sign question offers the pack's asks.md questions for that Moment while the caps in code allow one, and picking one is the ask, never on a schedule: the question is the sign and its two answers fill the lane (the do's ask). The board decides how it ends: a tap on either half, a stroke when the question takes one, a newer Moment, or 45 s, and it says so in ev ask before the Moment's ended. The answer is a line for Jev, and the memory keeps it: an answer that keeps becomes a tag for good, another for 30 days. Boop counts it as in progress until the device says it's finished.

Where the pieces join

Each package has its own vocabulary: agent-hooks has AgentEvents, MellowHarness has Events and lines, LinkKit has state, do and ev. Boop translates between them in the places below. Everything sent to the device goes out through Runtime, which is split by job over seven files in app/BoopKit/App/: Runtime.swift, and Runtime+Brain, +Buddies, +Device, +Talk, +Status and +Dev.
