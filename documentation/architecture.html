The model: Doing, Mood, Moment

Everything Boop shows is one of three layers on the board, and everything it says goes out on one of three channels. The words here are the ones the code, the prompt and the docs use (documentation/words.md has each one, the words it replaced and the names kept on the wire).

Four ideas hold the rest up. The agents: sessions, turns, calls, checks, helpers, to-dos and needs you. You: what you do to Boop (touch, words, presence, the button). Time, two clocks: how long (working or resting, the tiers) and what day (the part of the day, Boop's 5:00 day, special days, growth). Tags and dressing: tags are facts true now (Friday, busy, a party), and dressing is how Doing shows them (faster typing, headphones while music plays). The gel wears nothing for the night or a special day, its lids say the night, and there is nothing in the sky or the air; a pastime is the body's own move with nothing brought out beside the face: the screen's rule, written where the renderer decides it (the pack's render/gel/layers.h and pastimes.h). Its eyes are part three (gaze.h): they rest forward and turn to one thing at a time, up at you and then to the Mac's side when something needs you (macleft or macright, from where you showed it it sits), up at you as a long stretch of your typing stops, to your pointer while it moves (state.ptr), down the page as it reads along with you, to the screen as you watch a video, and, while it types at work, to a word as it starts to fall, with a rest between; in the other three activities the pose has its eyes.

The rules

  1. Three authors, one shape. A Moment is made by the board (a touch, played at once), the rules with no pass (a bye, an unlock's hello, a level-up, onboarding's acts) or a pass, which the rules brain answers and Jev may improve. The board's and the rules' own Moments carry no emotion of their own: they show the mood. Onboarding's finale is the one that does: it cheers in happy. When Jev can't be reached, the pass plays on the rules' answers.
  2. Boop's own beat: the idle beat. At an idle check-in Boop may hold up one thing, and code deals it (IdleBeat): the first that's due of an agent waiting on you (the waiting notice), the wish, another notice, an ask, a remembered line, a musing. One cap holds them all: at most one an hour and four a Boop day; on Quiet, and while a wish is open, only the first. Each kind keeps only the rest that stops a visible repeat (a notice a day for each agent, a wish one a day and never while cross, an ask 30 days once answered, a line 14 days, a musing not the sign shown last). Jev is asked one thing about it: hold this up, or nothing. An idle check-in needs you at the Mac and recent activity; with no agent open it comes at twice the personality's gap (TranscriptView.noAgentsGapTimes); it comes every 15–30 minutes on Normal (the personality's pace) and 30–60 on Quiet (Talk.pace), and only within two hours of something happening.
  3. A mood change is seen as soon as Boop shows its mood. The state carries the face the mood rests in (cross's is its level's: annoyed, grumpy or angry), and the face crosses to it. On the gel the body gives a small beat at a change of mood, one of three, and none for a change of face within one mood; it plays at once, under a Moment that has no emotion of its own. A Moment with its own emotion holds it, and the beat runs from that emotion when it ends. Asleep, with no app or under the needs-you screen there is no beat: the new mood's art is there when Boop wakes or the screen goes.
  4. Emotion within mood. A Moment's emotion is free within the mood's steering (a grumpy Boop's grudging proud at a win). It lasts the Moment's expression, then Doing shows in the mood again.
  5. One priority, top first: listening and its reply; needs you; Moments; Doing.
    • Listening cuts a playing Moment and drops the ones waiting. A reply plays over needs you.
    • A new needs-you cuts the playing Moment with its voice, sign and ask; Moments waiting are refused as they reach the front.
    • Moments play in the order they arrive (LinkKit's queue): at most four wait (the least time left goes first), each at most its time to live, and a newer one replaces a resting one only when nothing waits. The rules' Moments play only when nothing busy plays, else they're dropped.
    • Doing changes under a Moment and shows when it ends.
  6. Two sizes, two facts. Jev's size gives a Moment its loops (1 to 3) and its voice's kind (a sound, a word, a phrase). A turn's length gives its finish's size, by the turn's tier: small under tier 1 (a minute), medium from 1, big from 2 (5 minutes); a finish bigger than small plays once. A finish with no turn is sized by its loops, and a sad Boop's finish is small.
  7. Several agents, one Doing. Working if any agent works; else, with none open, resting while you're at the Mac and asleep once you're away, the screen is locked or you've been still past dozing off (an hour by day, 10 minutes at night; Core.sleepsAlone, from the Mac's idle time); with agents open, asleep when all have rested past dozing off and none waits on you; else resting. Needs you is the screen on top, not a Doing. The activity shown is that of the working session with the latest event; within a session the highest wins of four, in the order running (a command, the tests, a build, helpers at work), looking (a read, a search, the web), typing (an edit, any other tool, a plan), waiting. The gel shows each with its whole body: sat back with its paws together, leant in to the monitor, upright over the keys, slumped. A higher activity shows at once and a lower one after the one showing has had its time, 3 s for running and looking and 1.5 s for the others, so reads between edits don't rock the body; a call quiet for 20 s shows as waiting. The strip counts the rest, the to-do shown is the longest turn's, and the drawer lists every agent.
  8. Quiet takes most of the voice. Talk is quiet or normal. Quiet takes the swears, says nothing at a small Moment or a finish, deals no idle beat but an agent waiting on you, and offers no sign for a calm prompt that doesn't name Boop; the emotion, the size and the other signs go on. "Shh", the hush reply and the popover's Hush make Talk quiet for half an hour, then it's the setting's again; talking to Boop, the popover's button or choosing a Talk ends it sooner (Core.quiet). Swearing is a switch of its own in Settings, off unless you turn it on: off, no skit swears and no swear take plays. Face down is do not disturb: Moments are refused (but listening), the voice, sign and ask showing end, and the screen dims; Doing goes on, and the needs-you screen still shows bright.
  9. A work act is Doing. A command worth naming by rule (the tests, the build, a push), for as long as it runs: it's true in state (wact), and the result ends it. The board shows a low sign, what's running and then pass or fail, for at least 900 ms running and 2.6 s at its result, and Boop hops at a pass and flinches at a fail; the desk and the work stream stay. One at a time; a needs-you or listening holds it out of state, and it comes back still running. Its start never wakes Jev, and of its results only a push's does; a failed check has its own line.
  10. The work stream is Doing. A word coming forward is a do (call) queued next with 1.5 s to live, with no emotion or voice. It ends as soon as it starts, so it never holds a Moment up, and it is sent only while the stream shows (not under needs you or listening). In a full queue the least time left goes first, so a word never pushes out a Moment with longer to wait.

The lane

The bottom band (Behaviour::laneBubble and caption) is a rule, with the drawer's grabber in its middle while there are agents to list, and one row under it, which shows one thing at a time: the bubble while the voice plays, or an ask's two answers when nothing needs you, in warm white; else the narration's words as a caption, in the large font and a light grey, never as a bubble, which stays 2.5 s after the words end (under needs you, only a reply's; none while an ask's answers show); else the strip: the needs-you name first, then a finish's names, then the counts, in words when they fit ("2 working · 1 idle", the to-do list's "3/7" at the right end) and as marks when a name takes the room. Once a finish's words end, by 3 s at the latest, the gel's low sign rises into the row instead, the strip fading as it comes, and names its thread on one line, for the 5 s it lingers; Boop's whole face stays clear. Nothing a person must read on the gel's board is under the 22 px font; the 13 px one is for art.

An agent's card. An agent may hand Boop a card to hold (Card): a status while its turn runs ("fixing the overflow", with its step and its own guess of the minutes left, counted down as given and never corrected) and a result for 15 s after the turn ("nav overflow fixed", a detail, and maybe a link). It comes from boop-cards, a tool server the agent calls, as one line on the hook socket with the agent's process, which is how the core finds its session (Core.card); like a hook it only reports, returns at once and works with Boop closed. On the board a status is no card of words: while the agent the screen follows works, the board holds a live picture of its work instead (pic: its plan's steps, its changes), and the status's words go to the popover's row and the drawer; only a result shows on the board as a card (Core.cardShow). A show, a picture the agent drew (Show), outranks both: the board holds it up for 30 s once its turn has ended, counting only the time the board is free to hold it up, not while a Moment plays or waits there or something needs you (Core.tidyBoardShows). A card is part of Doing, with the work stream: needs you, a Moment, a sign and a work act all cover it, and it comes back after. With several, Boop holds the card of the agent you last prompted, or the one you swiped to, and another agent's status never takes it; only a result does, each once, in the order they finished, for at least 8 s (Cards). In the drawer, a row's second line is its card's words while it has one. A result needs no putting away: it goes after its 15 s, or at that session's next prompt, and one that ended while you were away waits for you. The words are the agent's: they reach the screen, the popover's row and the transcript, and no line Jev reads. A result may also carry the agent's part in Boop's mind (Card.lines, seemed, remember): up to two lines Boop might say, checked as a remembered line is, of which Boop picks one or none by its own rule (MomentRule.offeredLine: never a cheering line at a failure or while cross or sad) and holds it low after the finish; how the person seemed, which the rules brain counts as a fact the mood map already names; and a note for the dream's journal, which the dream judges. The agent learns all this from agent-brief.json, which Boop writes beside the tool server: who Boop is, what it remembers and how it feels, and nothing while Settings' "Your agent helps Boop think" is off.

Amber means needs you, and only that: its dot and name in the lane, its row in the drawer, and its card. The gel's needs-you card stands in the top band, filled amber, and Boop ducks under it: a mark and "NEEDS YOU", then what it asks of you, the state's attn.kind (approve: "a yes or no", question, plan: "a plan to read"), or "Continue on your Mac" from a Mac that doesn't say. Capitals are for the board's own cards (NEEDS YOU, LISTENING, NEW TASK); everything Boop holds, a sign or a low sign, is lower case ("pass", "level 12!"), and the Mac sends it so. Gold celebrates, as a shape or a celebration's low sign, never as the voice.

How Boop works, on one page

One ordinary event: Claude finishes a turn on "api".

  1. The hook. Claude Code's Stop hook runs agent-hook, which writes one JSON line to Boop's socket and never waits.
  2. Intake. The Mac reads the agent's last message into a reading (its kind, tone, length and up to three short phrases) and lets the words go. The finish and the reading go into today's transcript file.
  3. The rules, at once. The core updates the sessions and sends the board a new state: Doing, the mood, the counts, the to-do list and the tags. The work stream sends its own word for the finish ("done").
  4. The view. TranscriptView writes the event as a line for the brain, counts runs (quick wins, failing checks) and decides whether it wakes the brain.
  5. The brain, later. One brain with seats (SeatedBrain). The rules brain answers every question at once, with no network (RulesBrain): the mood, the emotion, and sometimes a sign; to your words, the reply; at a skit with more than one, which take. It reads the event's facts, not the prompt, moves the mood only by the pack's mood map (MoodMap), and never shows the same face twice running for one kind of moment. With a key, Jev reads the prompt (below) and answers the same questions in one request, all multiple choice; its answer replaces the rules' in its seat (the mood, the face, a reply and its sign, a skit's take) only if it comes within the pass's deadline and is one of the options. Slow, failing or absent, it changes nothing: the pass plays on the rules' answers, and the popover tells the key's owner. Never whether Boop speaks.
  6. The outputs. The mood output keeps or moves the mood. The react output applies its code rules (Talk, needs you), picks a recorded take, adds the narration, and sends a do to the board.
  7. The board. LinkKit's queue plays it when the Moment before it ends. The board picks the design's variation, sizes a finish by the turn's length, plays the take, and reports ended. Between Moments it deals its pastimes by the tiers.

Watching it: in debug mode every step above lands in debug.jsonl, the transcript's events, the view's lines, each pass with its prompt and answers, and every message sent to the board. boopctl dash reads only that file: Boop now with the CONTEXT Jev last read, then the mood and memory, the rules' Moments and Jev's, side by side.

Where decisions are made: agent-hooks (which hooks count, needs you); the prompt reader (readings); the core (Doing, needs you, the tags and tiers, quiet for a while, BOOT's line, the drawer); the work stream (which word plays); the view (lines, runs, what wakes the brain, the check-ins); the mood's rules (its fade on the clock, and how cross it is); the Moment's rules (MomentRule: every Moment's move and size, and the whole Moment at your lock or unlock, a working check-in and a boop from the world; Skits: what it says; InviteLedger: whether a skit invites a touch; Remarks: whether a skit carries a remark, against your usual); the idle beat's dealing (IdleBeat); the rules brain, and Jev in its seats when it answers in time (the mood, the emotion and the sign of every other Moment, the reply to your words, and a skit's take); the react output's code (silences, the signs offered, takes); the board (the queue, variations, pastimes, what a touch is); and the pack (the moods' prose and fades, every look and sound).

Memory

  • The transcript is every event, a file a day (Transcript). The view rebuilds what the brain hears from its last 24 hours.
  • Two readers of the transcript for the mind, each with its checkpoint. The day's (MemoryReader) keeps today as it goes, a calendar day from 5:00 that triggers nothing: your company (when you last touched or talked to Boop, came back), the tallies for CONTEXT's Today row. Growth (GrowthStore) counts each event's XP as it's logged, once, by its line's facts: a turn whose finish is a success, a ship, a check fixed, or your time with Boop (Growth.source). It keeps today's XP by source, at most 300 a Boop day and 30 of it care, with the ring full at 100; the last week's rings; the days in a row with a full ring; and the level, 1 + XP / 500, which never goes down. A level or the ring's closing is celebrated once the board shows its moment: with the board out of reach it waits for its hello, and one the board skips or a request cuts is due again. A shake earns none. A level-up and the ring closing are the core's rule moments (Core.grew), held while something needs you, and one moment when they come together: the ring's sign, today's done!, held up, and the level held low in gold (the do's gold, level 13!) with the gel's rim glowing gold; either alone is that half. Every level-up is the same moment: Boop's body is one size at every level. The dream's (DreamReader) keeps everything since the last dream, however long: the tallies and the journal of notable lines.
  • A dream updates the memory from everything since its checkpoint (Memory.dream): it makes the dream, the span in tags and a sentence, which Boop tells the next time you show up ("Boop dreamed about Thursday to Saturday: …"), told only once its moment has played; it runs the dream's write; and it moves the checkpoint. Hooks reach only a running app, so a Mac closed for days dreams once of what was recorded. It dreams when you press d on the dashboard; by itself once a sleep, after Boop has been asleep with no agent working for 2 hours from a start at night or 5 from a start by day (you away, or dozed off); and at once when it hasn't in two days. Each only with something new, one at a time. D on the dashboard previews it, the dream and the write's edits, and keeps nothing.
  • The daily counts, daily-counts.jsonl, a third reader of the transcript (CountsReader): a row a day of numbers and the date, never words, threads or notes. The dashboard's w shows the week, and Boop --counts prints a summary you may choose to send. Nothing leaves the Mac by itself. The board's link coming and going is a log-only event, link, for these.
  • The invite ledger, a fourth reader of the transcript (InviteReader, its state InviteLedger): the invites asked, when the day's were, and each pose's answers and misses in a row, folded from the reaction records and the board's invite events, kept in the store and caught up at launch, so it's never a store of its own.
  • Your usual, a fifth reader of the transcript (NormalsReader, its state Normals): how long your turns take (the last 50 wins with calls), the hour you first show up (the last 14 Boop days), how long your breaks are (the last 30 of 10 minutes to 4 hours), your wins a day, your best run of wins, and the skits since the last remark, folded from the lines' facts and the reaction records. Short lists, so a median is a sort; kept in the store and caught up at launch like the others. A launch's catch-up has no lines, so what it reads back of the turns and the hellos is only what the checkpoint already had.
  • The written memory, memory.md, is short notes in three sections and a line for each dream's day. At each dream code proposes its notes from counted days and the journal, and a model answers small questions: your own Claude Code or Codex, run unseen with no tools, no hooks and no session kept, while Settings' "Your agent helps Boop think" is on (AgentToolModel), else Apple Intelligence, about what you said (MemoryProposer, MemoryPipeline); the checks hold every edit (MemoryEdits). What it counts from is a digest of each dream, memory-days.json: the last 60, numbers and what you said to Boop that isn't on its private list and holds no secret, kept on the Mac only. Your words in a note or the day line pass the same gates as a moment: not private, by the list and the model, no order and no secret, so with no model they wait. A field the file doesn't have takes its default, and a file that won't read moves aside to memory-days.json.broken, which Forget also removes. Each is dated by the Boop day of your last event in it, so a Friday that ends at 01:00 is Friday's. "Forget" empties You, Your work and Recently and what they came from (Memory.forgetAboutYou): the span's journal and counts, the digests' words, hours, threads and tones, the quoted notes gone from added, the lines of notes gone, and older copies of memory.md. You and Boop stays, with the counts of care and of how much you were here. A note deleted stays gone: it's in added and not in the file. A note the dream updated or let go isn't deleted. You can read and edit it, and the popover shows it through the memory store: a few notes on its Boop tab, and all of them in its What Boop knows pane, each with a pin, which keeps it, and a cross, which forgets it. Jev reads it as MEMORY. Only MemoryStore reads and writes the files.
  • Memory you feel. At the dream's write each new note also gets a line in the pet's voice, at most 40 printable ASCII characters, held on the low sign (MemoryPipeline.sign, on this Mac; a note whose line the checks refuse has none, and one the model gave no answer for is asked again at the next dream). One is dealt as an idle beat when nothing before it is due, the line shown longest ago first, and the same line not again for 14 days (Memory.felt); Jev says whether to hold it up. It plays as a little look (the pack's reply-remember clip), the pack's word through Jev's say (for now the bank's "Hey", until a "hey!" is recorded), and the line on the low sign, so the face stays clear. The Mac's narration gives the note: "Boop remembers you stay up late on Fridays."

The wish

Boop's want, one a Boop day: pet, chat or work, each met by what you do anyway. Code deals it as an idle beat (IdleBeat): once the agent waiting on you has had its notice, at an idle check-in with no wish made yet today and Boop not cross, the kinds it may want taking turns by the day; Jev says only whether to hold it up, and 173-a-workday watches the day. Code holds the rest (Core+Wish.swift): one open, work only while no agent works, chat only once push-to-talk is authorized, never once Boop has dozed off, never on Quiet. It's kept as wish_start and wish_end (met, gaveup, closed), read back on launch. What meets it does so at once, by a rule, with the board's wish_met, and Jev then hears it: for a pet, a touch on its face (Touch.meetsPetWish: a poke, a stroke, a hold; a touch's own answer stays, under the heart), for a chat push-to-talk, for work a prompt you send or a request you answer. While it waits the board shows a thought bubble (state.wish) and the wait counts only while you're here, it hasn't dozed off and nothing needs you. The Mac's sleep and Boop not running don't count (Core.wishTickGapMs), an away counts up to its since, and a launch reads the wait back from the events. With no Mac the board takes the bubble down, and during a full act. Asleep only because no agent is open isn't dozing: it may still want something. CONTEXT's You row says an open wish and how long it has waited; waiting moves no mood. Code takes it down at three hours (wish_gone, which wakes Jev) and closes it quietly at 5:00. Wish lines are notable in the journal, so the written memory carries the pattern.

It noticed

Code finds what's true about your day (Notices). A notice never makes a new moment where one exists: it adds a fact to that moment. Your coming back says what finished while you were gone, in CONTEXT's Noticed row, when its hello has no special day's sign and no dream to tell (a hello's one extra); your first prompt today on a thread you'd left says how long it's been; your goodnight's caption says the day's turns (and a [bye night] sign may); a month, 100 days or a year are special days as any other, and the day's first hello's caption keeps to the day and the dream. A wish's narration says its reason, once Boop is two weeks old: you haven't said hi, or your morning stroke is an hour past your usual time on 5 of the last 7 days.

New moments come only at an idle check-in, as the idle beat: waiting first of everything, the others after the wish, under the beat's one cap, never while something needs you; on Quiet and over an open wish only waiting. waiting, an agent idle from 15 min to an hour, a day's rest per agent; stretch, 2 h at the Mac with no break, where a launch or 2 h with no event is a break, a day's rest; rough, 3 turns ended failed within the hour or a sharp prompt, a day's rest, where Boop leans in close; late, past midnight after 3 of the last 7 nights past 1:00, a week's rest, silent, and the only one that comes once Boop has dozed off. Each is a pure function over the digests and the moment. It counts once its moment has played, as a dream told does (Memory.recordNotice). When one plays Boop looks up at you first (notice's animation; rough's lean instead), and its short reason is in the board's lane and whole on the Mac. Its sign is the pack's line ([notice waiting]), which Jev holds up or not.

The world

Every buddy that joins lives in one world: a small server (internal/world/, Hono on Vercel, Postgres) and a client on the Mac (app/BoopKit/World/). WorldLink, on the runtime's queue, joins once onboarding's world screen has been shown, then sends the buddy's state (World.State, and nothing else) on a change of what it's doing, a level-up or the ring closing, with the counts batched for up to 3 minutes, a heartbeat every 10 and "asleep" at once on a lock or a quit. The gestures waiting come back in a send's reply, or from a check every 10 minutes with nothing sent, and on an unlock. The world itself is fetched only while the World tab is open, every 30 s.

A gesture from another buddy becomes a world event in the transcript (WorldGestures): the rules answer it with no pass (MomentRule.world), always with a warm small face and a sign from the pack's [world] lines, proud and medium for a cheer, a curious look when cross, and the narration says who and where, by region only. What one reply brings is one moment, so gestures that come together all play; at most 3 such moments of boops, waves and hearts an hour play, the rest logged as not playing and counted on the World tab; one ship's cheers are one moment; at night, while you're away, or while something needs you, they wait, and come back as one line. Off, the buddy is deleted from the world: you can still look, but nobody can boop you.

Narration and asks

Narration is why the buddy does what it does, in plain words (Narration). The Mac renders it from the event the brain answered, never the brain: a sentence for the popover and the debug log, and a few words for the board's lane (the do's reason). Every kind of event has one, or is listed in Narration.silent with why not, and a test holds to that.

The rules' own moments carry one too: a met wish. Where nothing plays, a react holding only a reason says it, which records no line, so the brain hears nothing new: falling asleep and waking (quiet for 1 h), a real mood change with no fact to show (Boop's grumpy now, not a fade). A touch the gel's board answers itself says why itself as it plays, before any Mac or Jev (you poked Boop, app/touch_reason.h, in the Mac's words); the Mac's reason for it, in the same words within 10 s, keeps that caption up if it's still showing and shows nothing if it's gone. With a name you gave it, state's buddy tells the board.

Pastimes and the moves between Doings get no caption, nor does dressing: the popover says them (Boop's stretching to pass the time.), asking the mirror what its face is doing and reading the pack's mac/passing.tsv. That's the popover's mirror's own, which deals its own pastimes, not the board's.

An ask is a question the buddy holds up with two answers you tap in the lane, from the pack's asks.md (Asks). It never needs an answer, and the agents never wait on one. The board says how it ended (ev ask), and the line goes into HISTORY; a question marked keeps is asked only once, ever.

When moments meet

A moment plays whole, then the next: what comes meanwhile waits in line until its time runs out, and only a needs-you or your own hand and voice cut in. The queue (LinkKit's, linkkit/SPEC.md §4) and BoopDevice.play hold it; CollisionTests and firmware's test_behaviour pin it.

The morning. The first moment of your day, or the one that tells the dream, is a greeting (Moment.greeting). Its sign waits until its words end. The work stream waits until it's over: no term in the strip, no word forward, no act showing, and an act already running comes back still running. A special day's sign is code's, on that moment, whatever Jev picks of feeling; it wins over the dream's sign, and the dream is still told in the narration. The narration leads with Boop's day count.

Transitions. From one Doing to another the gel plays a short clip instead of a cut. They hold nothing up: the new look has already come, and anything urgent cuts through them. A word forward, or a reaction, is the moment then.

Two that bend the rule, and stay: a finished turn's name outranks an act's low sign in the lane; and words take the lane from an act's low sign while they're said. A remembered line takes the lane from an act's low sign, and a finished turn's name from it.

The rules that are easy to break

  • Decisions and effects are separate. The core and the brain decide. Outputs (mood, react) carry out effects and check their own rules.
  • Everything goes through the transcript. Hooks, pokes, check-ins and everything the rules and outputs do are raw events in one shape; the view folds them into what the brain hears (app/BoopKit/Core/TranscriptView.swift). Anything else is a log line. You at the Mac (Hands, Along) is no event: only tags and ptr on the board, which nothing in the transcript, the brain, the world or the daily counts reads.
  • The packages stay apart. agent-hooks, MellowHarness and LinkKit know nothing of Boop or of each other. Boop translates between them in a few places (documentation/architecture.html, Where the pieces join), and anything Boop-specific stays in app/ or firmware/.
  • The harness is generic. MellowHarness takes events, asks every output's questions in one request and hands each output its own answers. It never reads an event's facts, picks what Boop says or talks to the device.
  • Only Voice knows what Boop can say. Only the memory store reads and writes the memory files. Only LinkKit knows Bluetooth or USB: on the Mac its DeviceLink and transports, with Boop's vocabulary on them in app/BoopKit/DeviceLink/BoopDevice.swift; on the board its device library, which firmware/src/main.cpp feeds the serial port's messages.
  • The brain is never on the screen's path. Rules keep the screen true at once (the look, "needs you", the tap's poke, the rules' Moments). Every Moment that needs judging, a finished turn's included, is the brain's, later or not at all.
  • The rules always answer; Jev only takes their seats. What wakes the brain: your prompt's reading, a finish, your words, a failed check or its fix, a touch, your coming back, an answered ask, a wish's end, a push's result, an answered request, and Boop's own idle check-in (TranscriptView.wakes). The rules brain answers every pass; Jev, with a key, may replace its answer in a seat (SeatedBrain.seats: the mood, the emotion, a sign where one is offered, the reply to your words, and a skit's take), in time and with one of the options, or not at all. Whatever was a fixed table is a rule in code, in one place (MomentRule, pinned by MomentRuleTests): a Moment's move and size, and the whole Moment at your lock or unlock, a working check-in and a boop from the world; when it speaks (Skits, pinned by SkitTests); and which one thing an idle check-in may hold up (IdleBeat). A turn's start alone wakes nothing.
  • The brain answers multiple choice only. The rules and Jev answer the same questions, Jev about the prompt, in plain text; there's no free text. Keep questions few, with options that say what they're not.
  • The steering files are read-only at runtime. The pack's steering/ is the single source, and the app bundles it at build time (Package.swift copies it into the app's resources).
  • No code, commands, tool output, file contents or agent transcripts go to the brain. Of your prompt and of the agent's last message only the reading goes, each with up to three short phrases of it; the only other words are what you say to Boop on push-to-talk, cut short. Both texts stay on the Mac: app/BoopKit/Prompt/ scrubs each, reads it (with Apple Intelligence on macOS 26 while it's on, else by counting only) and passes on only its reading; nothing keeps them.
  • The world gets World.State and nothing else. The buddy's name and level, its UTC offset, one of six activities, counts, its days in a row and its colour (WorldTests pins the fields); nothing before onboarding's world screen, nothing with the world off (above).
  • "Needs you" and the screen priority are plain rules in the core. When moments meet, one plays whole, then the next (above).
  • The device renders, reports, and decides when a Moment plays (LinkKit's queue), never what Boop does: the Mac sends each thing to play at once and never times it. It receives the same messages over Bluetooth and USB. Its drawing code stays independent of the display library, so the simulator and a later ESP-IDF + LVGL port can reuse it.
  • The Mac's face is the board's firmware. The popover and onboarding show the gel through the pack's mirror of the board (characters/boop/mac/mirror/board.js, in SlimeBuddy), the firmware built to WebAssembly and checked in: a change to what the gel board draws, here or in the pack, rebuilds it (firmware/tools/mirror.sh) into the pack, pushed with the change, and make -C internal mirror fails until it does. It's fed what the board gets and never answers.

The shape

Boop is a Mac app plus a small screen on your desk. Data flows one way: from your coding agents (Claude Code, Codex), through the Mac app, to the device. Boop only watches and reports. It never approves or blocks anything an agent does. Three packages handle the general parts (reading agent hooks, asking the multiple-choice model, talking to the device), and they know nothing about Boop or each other. Boop's own code connects them.

Who the creature is, its moods, prompts, faces and voice, is a character pack, which the engine never reads the meaning of (characters/CHARACTER.md). Boop's character is a repository of its own, with this one as its engine/ submodule: SlimeBuddy, the gel slime. Only what's the character's own goes there; everything a character could share is here.

A touch that began on the card Boop holds is the card's and nothing of Boop's: "kind": "card", did next or prev for a finger across it (28 px or more), which the core answers with another card in the next state (Core.swipeCard), or open for a tap, which opens the result's link or the session's thread on the Mac (Core.tapCard). With two agents or more, the screen follows one (Core.watched): the state's pips (a kind and a lane slot each, "w0i-c1"), watch (which pip), who (its name, in its pip on the strip's line, up to 28 bytes, cut with "…") and, for 4 s, pill ([pip, "api finished"], news from another); a swipe across the desk then says "kind": "agent", did next or prev, which the core answers by following another (Core.swipeAgent), kept as yours while it works.

Each package has its own vocabulary: agent-hooks has AgentEvents, MellowHarness has Events and lines, LinkKit has state, do and ev. Boop translates between them in the places below. Everything sent to the device goes out through Runtime, which is split by job over seven files in app/BoopKit/App/: Runtime.swift, and Runtime+Brain, +Buddies, +Device, +Talk, +Status and +Dev.
